Four near misses. One typical month. Any pharmacy.

- Amiodarone on the script, amlodipine in your hand — the names, again. Caught at the final check.
- The wrong strength of atorvastatin, already in the bag. Spotted at handout.
- Two patients, same surname — the wrong script almost went out the door.
- A methotrexate label reading "daily" instead of "weekly." Caught, thank goodness.

Each one: caught, fixed, a breath out, back to the queue.

Then review time comes.

We flick through the near-miss book. "Bit of a few this month — watch the look-alikes." Nods around the room. Meeting done.

Next month, amiodarone and amlodipine trip someone up again. Because we treated four warnings as four lucky escapes — not as one pattern pointing straight at where the next real error is coming from.

We're excellent at catching near misses. We're just not built to learn from them before the month runs out.

The near miss isn't the failure. Forgetting it by review time is.

What's the one that keeps coming back in yours?
