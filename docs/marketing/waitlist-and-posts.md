## 3. General LinkedIn post (no product mention — post this first)

"No harm done."

Three words we say in the dispensary more often than we'd like to admit.

The wrong strength, spotted at the final check. The look-alike box, caught before the bag was sealed. The typo, noticed just before the label printed.

Every one of them is a near miss — an error that didn't reach the patient. And every time, the temptation is the same: breathe out, fix it, move on.

But here's what I've come to believe after years behind the bench: a near miss isn't a lucky escape. It's information. It's the same look-alike packaging, the same understaffed afternoon, the same interruption at the worst possible moment — quietly telling you where the next real error is going to come from.

The safest pharmacies I know aren't the ones with fewer near misses. They're the ones that talk about them openly, without blame, and actually change something because of them.

That's the hard part. Not catching the near miss — we're good at that. It's writing it down, seeing the pattern, and fixing the cause before it catches someone out.

How does your pharmacy handle near misses? Do you capture them — or do they disappear the moment they're fixed?
