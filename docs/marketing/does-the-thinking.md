# "It does the interpreting for you" (angle: easy to understand)

Content on how the app turns raw near-miss data into plain-English insight, so
you don't need to be a data person. Good as a Facebook/LinkedIn post or a reply.

---

One thing I really wanted to get right: you shouldn't need to be a numbers person to use it.

A logbook just gives you a pile of entries — it's on you to work out what matters. This does that part for you, and puts it in plain English:

- Instead of a list, it tells you: "Atorvastatin came up 4 times — same look-alike box as last month."
- Instead of you working out whether things are improving, it says: "Metoprolol mix-ups dropped from 5 to 1 since your shelf change."
- Instead of guessing where to focus, it points you straight to the one problem that keeps coming back.

You're not reading numbers and trying to make sense of them — you're reading sentences that already make sense. Anyone on the team can pick up the report and get it straight away.

That was the whole idea: let it do the thinking, so your time goes on the fix — not the spreadsheet.
