Every pharmacist I know agrees near-miss reporting matters. And almost every one of them finds it a chore.

Think about what it actually takes:

**Recording** — you catch a near miss mid-dispense, and now you're meant to stop, find the logbook or the spreadsheet, and write it up. In the middle of a queue. So half of them never get logged.

**Reviewing** — end of the month, someone sits down with a page of scribbled entries and tries to make sense of them. Which drug? How many times? Was it the same look-alike box twice?

**Reporting** — then you turn all that into something you'd be comfortable showing an inspector, and into an actual plan for the team meeting.

Three separate jobs, all fiddly, all landing on someone already flat out. No wonder it slips.

But here's the thing — the whole point of that chore is to stop the next error reaching a patient. That's not admin. That's the job.

So if there were a way to make recording take ten seconds, have the review add itself up for you, and turn the month into a ready-to-read report — wouldn't that be worth a look? Not to add another system. To make the one thing that actually protects patients something people will actually use.

That's what I've been building. Early look here if it's useful: https://tally.so/r/D4YgQl

Honest question: how much friction is there in the way YOU record near misses right now?
