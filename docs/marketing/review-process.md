# The review process — "what you actually see" (explainer)

Grounded in what appears on screen at each step: log -> manager reviews & decides
each -> generate -> report on screen -> meeting. Per-incident decisions happen
during review; the meeting is for bigger changes + sign-off. Good as a Facebook
post or a "how does it work?" reply.

---

A few people asked how it works, so here's what you'd actually see, step by step:

Logging one (any staff, through the month): you open the app and tap through a few quick options — what happened, which medicine, where it was caught. No typing paragraphs. Hit save and you're done in seconds. No name attached.

Reviewing them (the manager, before the meeting): you see the whole month's near misses in one list. Tap one and it shows a suggested fix in plain English, with three buttons — accept it, reword it, or "no change needed." You work down the list; most take a couple of seconds each. The AI only suggests — you make every call.

Generating the report: press one button. A moment later a full report appears on screen, already written in plain English — nothing to format, nothing to add up.

What the report looks like — it's laid out like a meeting, top to bottom:
- The month at a glance — how many, up or down on last month
- Did last month's changes work? — each old problem marked gone, less, or still happening
- This month's near misses — each with the action you chose
- A sign-off line at the bottom

At the meeting: you just read straight down the report on the screen (or print it). Everyone can follow it, because it's plain words — not a page of numbers.

Then the bit that's on you, not the app: someone actually makes the change — moves the shelf, adds the warning label, tweaks the process. The app can't do that part. But it does close the loop: next month's report tells you whether the change worked, so you're not guessing.
