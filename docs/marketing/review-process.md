# The review process + what's generated (explainer)

Plain-English, logical walk-through with the manager REVIEW step included:
log -> manager reviews & decides each near miss -> generate report -> meeting.
Per-incident decisions are made during review; the meeting is for system-level
changes only. Good as a Facebook post or a "how does it work?" reply.

---

A few people asked how it actually works, so here's the whole thing, start to finish:

1. Through the month — anyone on the team logs a near miss as it happens. A few taps, no names, takes seconds.

2. Before the meeting, the manager reviews them — you go through the month's near misses and decide what to do about each one. For every near miss it suggests a simple fix based on NZ best practice, and you either accept it, put it in your own words, or mark "no change needed." It's quick, because it's all in one place instead of scattered through a book — and the AI only suggests; you make every call.

3. Then you hit generate — about two minutes, and it turns the month into a plain-English report:
   - The month at a glance — how many, up or down on last month, where they're clustering
   - Did last month's changes work? — each old problem marked gone, happening less, or still happening
   - This month's near misses — each one with the action you decided
   - Sign-off — a line to confirm the review happened

4. At the meeting — you read it out, look at the patterns that keep coming back, and agree any bigger changes as a team, with a name and a date on them.

So the individual decisions are already made before you sit down — the meeting is for the bigger system changes and signing it off.
